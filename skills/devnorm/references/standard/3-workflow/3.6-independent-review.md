# 3.6 Independent review

A non-author primary reviewer independently reviews each formal task. Set review scope from the change and evidence. When prior review is required, first review the design, applicable models and test design under section 3.2. Complete applicable real-entry acceptance for the candidate. Simple tasks handled directly need no separate independent review.

## Review unit and primary reviewer

An implementation subtask, local commit, semantic review unit, and final combined candidate are different objects. A review unit contains complete behavior, tests, necessary documentation, and exact input identity sufficient for independent judgment. Several local commits may form one unit. Do not split shared invariants or omit related changes to narrow review artificially.

The primary reviewer starts independent review in a fresh session uninvolved in implementation. When reviewing a candidate, the reviewer inspects the task record, [review checklist](../1-principles.md#17-manual-review-checklist), complete diff, and evidence, and personally runs required critical paths. Reviewers did not implement the reviewed work and write only authorized conclusions and evidence; they do not fix while approving. Author logs may be cited with their actual executor, not represented as the reviewer's own re-verification.

For formal tasks, check the complete change for out-of-scope changes and inspect implementation where trusted evidence leaves a gap. Check the implementation and evidence at entry points, shared writes, transactions and external side effects that affect critical guarantees. If scope or conformance is unclear, expand the review and resolve the uncertainty before concluding. Keep test-diff protection, human review of test changes, applicable acceptance, and trusted full checks for the exact final combination.

When the design and test design were independently reviewed upfront, the original reviewer normally continues. Check the exact candidate for deviations from the approved design and confirm that the approved test scenarios and assertions were implemented. Reuse unchanged design decisions and still-valid evidence.

## Approval and blockers

Approve review when the exact candidate satisfies applicable mandatory standards, the approved design and acceptance criteria, and all required verification evidence is available. Verify actual evidence and the planned check results. Inspect implementation where evidence leaves a gap. Retain the current conclusion, open items, and original evidence in the existing task record.

Block the candidate only when it violates an applicable requirement above, a required check fails, or required verification evidence is missing. Identify the requirement, actual or unresolved impact, and observed deviation or missing evidence. Resolve uncertainty that could affect approval before concluding. Return confirmed blockers and evidence promptly. Useful investigation independent of the blocker may continue within the original budget.

Record style preferences, optional improvements, and findings that do not affect approval as nonblocking suggestions. The author does not have to reply to each, change the candidate, or submit it for another review. Check automated findings against source and evidence under the same criteria, and document the basis for false positives. An authorized owner decides requirements trade-offs, test protection, and safeguard reductions. Review conclusions and comments grant no permission for later operations.

## Incremental re-review and cumulative budget

Prefer the original primary reviewer for fixes. Inspect new differences, original findings, and affected behavior, and replay the original failure. Widen review when interfaces, permissions, environment, or baseline change, or when contamination or unknown impact exists. A qualified replacement may be used if independence, trustworthy context, or execution conditions are lost; preserve the handoff basis and previous conclusions.

If business semantics, an architecture constraint, a critical architecture assumption, a model assumption, a key acceptance commitment, or a material verification obligation changes substantively, invalidate the affected design approval and evidence. Return the affected design and test design for independent review before changing the dependent implementation. For code-only repairs within the approved design, retain its approval and bind the new candidate to new or still-valid evidence. Replay the original failure.

Declare review rounds, runtime limits, and resource budget in advance for the task. Accumulate failures, timeouts, recoveries, and resends; changing sessions or candidates or splitting commits does not reset them. Estimate from observed startup, necessary checks, and model execution for comparable tools; label missing data as an estimate and calibrate on a limited scope rather than copying example minutes or repeatedly producing partial work with short timeouts. If time is insufficient, report progress and remaining work and obtain approval before continuation; do not automatically extend budgets or change test timeouts. A task-specific count or time limit is not a universal project rule. If blockers remain at exhaustion, stop that action and notify the coordinator and requirements owner for a decision. Do not auto-approve, substitute author self-review, or freeze unrelated work.

## 3.6.1 Exact candidate and trusted re-verification

Run trusted full checks on the final combined candidate under the project's execution contract. Isolated unit passes cannot be assembled into a substitute. Re-verify in a clean copy of the exact inputs with the required environment, without unrelated author worktree changes. Git-dependent checks need verifiable Git comparison inputs. A Git-free copy needs a comparison snapshot bound to candidate identity and independently validated by trusted tooling. Missing or unverifiable baselines fail, not pass as an empty diff.

The adopting project maps quick, affected, full, test-diff protection, trusted toolchain, isolation, and dependency policies. This universal standard does not mandate DevNorm source-maintenance runtimes, branded CLIs, guard scripts, or personal cache paths. Missing required mappings or authorization mean not run and stop the dependent action. Do not temporarily install tools, elevate permissions, or bypass guards to obtain a pass. Isolate global ignores, local tool configuration, and untrusted executable entry points; an author's local pass is not trusted evidence.

Before reuse, check evidence input identity, scope, toolchain, commands, environment, and export method. Cite only evidence demonstrably still applicable and explain why. A new candidate cannot directly inherit an old candidate's approval. Rerun affected checks and the original failures being repaired in this round; widen coverage when impact is unknown. Preserve evidence for historical failures already validly replayed and closed under unchanged conditions, without replaying them for each role. The final exact combination needs trusted full-check success required by the project contract. Once the above validation establishes that an existing result still applies exactly, a role change, archive, or completion step alone does not require another run. An earlier pass cannot override a new required failure. Validated trusted machine evidence may be cited, but an author-editable success file is not a trusted result. The reviewer still personally executes required critical paths and never labels a citation as personal execution.

Retain actual commands, exit codes, executors, and accessible original evidence. Transcribe runner summaries with only the fields actually emitted; do not compute totals or invent total, skipped, or todo. Protect originals referenced by active tasks, open issues, critical failures, model boundaries, and final acceptance under project retention policy. Summaries navigate; they do not replace original failures or required personal execution.

## Actual behavior acceptance

Changes involving CLI/UI, identity, permissions, external input, concurrency, storage, recovery, installation packages, or runtime integration require verification through real public entry points. Distinguish actual execution and unverified scope across files, databases, processes, browsers, isolated applications, and real services. Missing environment or authorization is not run, not inapplicable. Explain inapplicability only when no corresponding runtime behavior exists, as with pure documentation.

Retain both machine protection and human inspection of test differences. Compare important states with applicable models and actual replays. Models do not prove host isolation, operational authorization, or correct execution of external systems. For failures record location, expectation, observation, and evidence; return to section 3.5 for repair and re-review under this section.
