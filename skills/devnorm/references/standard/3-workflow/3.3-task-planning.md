# 3.3 Task brief and work allocation

Simple tasks need no form. Formal tasks use one [six-field short brief](../../../assets/templates/task-short.md) and expand with applicable sections of the [full brief](../../../assets/templates/task.md) when needed. Reference the existing project execution contract, identifying goals, risk, scope, exact inputs, checks, authorization, and stop conditions without duplicating known facts. Check directory, branch, and existing changes. Assign one writer to shared interfaces, dependency locks, and generated output, and separate source, test data, and runtime output.

## One authoritative record

Maintain shared facts, current progress, open questions, and attributed review conclusions in a persistent, accessible project location. Before dispatch, confirm the recipient can read the required exact version. A handoff repository is optional, not a mandatory commit before every step or a reason for another review ledger. Temporary directories and session summaries do not replace required durable records and original evidence. If an existing record covers the six fields, reference it instead of rewriting the brief; add only material changes to acceptance, inputs, risk, authorization, ownership, or conclusions. Organize the next actions by verifiable outcomes, not a subtask, status update, or approval for every command. Additional reading, reruns, or reports need an uncovered requirement, change, or risk; use the original record rather than a separate necessity form.

Identify implementation subtasks, local commits, semantic review units, and the final combined candidate separately. A semantic unit contains complete independently assessable behavior, tests, and necessary documentation. Do not split mechanically by file or commit count, or split shared invariants out of review. A commit is not approval, and a unit's approval does not establish trusted full checks of a later exact combination.

## Execution and review allocation

Estimate work, dependencies, and useful parallel opportunities before comparing direct execution with delegation. Include briefing, execution, receiving, understanding, integration, review, and rework. Label estimates and unknowns. Do not delegate work requiring extensive reinterpretation or reconstruction on return. Small tasks, tightly coupled work, and overall design usually belong to one executor. Formal work has one qualified non-author primary reviewer, not extra agents for summaries or formal role division.

For necessary delegation, check capabilities, tools, quota, and project limits, and agree on [return conditions](../../../assets/templates/task.md#return-conditions-for-delegated-work). Unapproved scope changes, critical ambiguity, repeated failure, or budget exhaustion stop the affected action while preserving state. The coordinator decides whether to revise the design, implementation, environment, or allocation. Use existing authorization within scope and seek separate approval for out-of-scope actions. Do not resend unknown operations or reset budgets through new sessions; unrelated authorized work may continue.

| Check arrangement | Task requirements | Merge conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal task | One task record, applicable design and prior review, required behavior checks, one non-author primary reviewer and applicable acceptance | Trusted full checks and applicable acceptance of the exact final combination |
| Evidence reuse for identical inputs, with consolidated milestone checks | Demonstrate continued applicability of origins, source, rules, dependencies, samples, tools, and environment; retain original logs and label reuse rather than rerunning | The isolated exact combination still receives trusted full checks before a real merge; isolated successes cannot be assembled into combined approval |

Determine prior review and modeling under [design and test design review](3.2-requirements-and-design.md#design-and-test-design-review) and section 3.2.1. Cite exact review inputs and conclusions in the same task record.

Choose arrangements from section 3.1 change facts rather than technology names. Reference existing project waiting, rework, and review budgets. Agree missing applicable limits first, without copying model-example numbers. Failures, timeouts, recoveries, and resends accumulate. Exhaustion does not mean approval or permit author self-approval.

Re-review checks impact and replays original failures under section 3.6, expanding scope when needed. Formal tasks use the same independent review process. Reducing safeguards still requires the existing approval. For a new arrangement, agree trial scope, termination conditions, and comparable quality and total-cost measures before a bounded adoption decision. A short small-sample trial is not proof of general effectiveness.
