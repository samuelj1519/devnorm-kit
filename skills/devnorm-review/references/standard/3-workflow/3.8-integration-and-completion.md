# 3.8 Merge and completion

Before updating a project-designated integration target, verify authorization, exact candidate, target branch, inputs, and check configuration. This includes merge, integration cherry-pick/rebase/squash, and direct commits or pushes to that target. Ordinary task-branch commits use quick checks, not automatic full checks. A trusted check service or merge tool confirms all applicable tests, dependency checks, security scans, and no coverage decline. Execute the full command registered in the project contract. Author-edited success records and local quick checks are insufficient. Missing tools or unclear evidence stop merge.

Reusing an item of evidence requires proof that the source, rules, dependencies, samples, environment, security data, and coverage baseline it covers remain identical; old candidate approval does not transfer to a new candidate. Before consolidated checks, prepare the combined version only in an isolated directory or temporary index. Do not move the target branch or create a real merge commit. Run full checks and applicable acceptance for the exact combination, then merge. Changes, conflicts, or contamination return to section 3.5 and review of affected scope under section 3.6; expand scope for unknown effects. Semantic-unit success is not combination success. Direct tasks not updating an integration target need not run this step.

Commit only approved files with normal hooks and protect others' work. Correct shared history with new commits by default. Apply [section 6.6](../6-version-control/6.6-undo-and-history.md#66-undoing-changes-and-editing-history) to unshared editing, approved history replacement, and tags; ordinary commit or push authorization grants no exception. Check PRs, check statuses, and merge methods under [sections 6.4 and 6.5](../6-version-control/6.4-pushes-and-pull-requests.md#64-pushes-and-pull-requests). Installation, release, and push retain their own authorization. If merge succeeds but cleanup fails, report merged with completion blocked and notify the coordinator rather than claiming full completion. Record author-side checks passing, handover to independent re-verification, and overall completion separately. Planning a handover is not completion and does not by itself record `BLOCKED`.

## Records and evidence

Update the original authoritative task record with this increment, preserving implementer and primary-reviewer attribution: exact versions, commands, exit codes, initial failures and reruns, unverified scope, next owner, and necessary lessons. Do not repeatedly transcribe facts available from original records. Sufficient existing records do not require parallel development, acceptance, and review reports or an extra completion report. Preserve formal acceptance receipts and required signatures. Direct work needs only a brief result. An ordinary review adds only its conclusion and exact scope, confirmed findings, personally executed or cited evidence, and unverified limits, linking existing facts. Checklists guide coverage; they do not require copying each rule, normal result, or irrelevant N/A. Required unverified items, failures, and independent attribution still remain explicit.

Keep raw output separate from summaries. Prefer native runner summaries, exit codes, and failure locations. Read scoped portions of large logs, mark truncation or redaction, and link originals. Do not load every log into model context or add paid summarization by default. Summaries navigate evidence; they cannot hide required failures, replay obligations, or actual executors.

Feed rework, review findings, post-merge regressions, architecture violations, and duplicate-code growth into existing quality statistics. Keep unknowns unknown without adding a ledger for every ordinary repair. The record owner maintains the current conclusion and evidence links. Handover adds only changes, risks, and the next action; acknowledging it creates no new approval.

Before cleanup, verify exact paths, ownership, later consumers, references, retention conditions, and authorization. Evidence for active tasks, unresolved issues, critical failures, model boundaries, and final acceptance is formally retained. Referenced temporary material receives the same protection. Task closure or elapsed days does not automatically end retention. Retain material when policy is absent or continued use is uncertain. Verify archive access, index, and originals before authorized removal of rebuildable temporary copies. Keep others' resources, active resources, and unique evidence.

## Communication with users

Lead with the current conclusion or direct answer, then only this update's changes, important risks and unverified limits, and concrete user actions. End directly when the user has no action. Do not impose per-round status updates, time-report formats, or item limits. Link original evidence or expand on request instead of repeating standards and historical logs.

Expand sufficiently for requested explanations, complete lists, or reasoning. Retain every important risk, failure, and action even beyond five items; brevity does not justify omission. Distinguish author checks passed, independent review complete, merged/accepted, and installed/loaded by their actual status. Request only the exact missing authorization; silence is not approval.
