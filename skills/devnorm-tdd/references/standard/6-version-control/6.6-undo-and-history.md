# 6.6 Undoing changes and editing history

Distinguish uncommitted content, your unshared commits, shared history, and published tags. If ownership, dependents, authorization, or the exact version is unclear, stop affected writes and preserve the state. Do not use destructive commands merely to obtain a clean working tree.

- **Uncommitted content:** undo only your changes within the specified scope. Preserve others' staged, unstaged, and untracked work. `reset --hard`, `clean`, and forced worktree removal can lose data and are not routine troubleshooting steps.
- **Your unshared commits with no dependents:** amend or rebase only when the project permits it, ownership is confirmed, and existing commit authorization covers the work. Preserve necessary recovery references. A changed SHA or content requires renewed diff, check, and review assessment; do not reuse approval of the old candidate.
- **Shared or protected history:** prefer a new corrective commit or `revert`. Necessary rewriting needs explicit approval for the exact repository, branch, scope, and impact, coordinated dependents, and recovery evidence. Ordinary commit or push authorization does not imply permission to rewrite.
- **Approved remote history replacement:** approval fixes the remote, target ref, and source commit. Verify the actual old remote SHA immediately before acting and update only the named ref. Use `--force-with-lease=<ref>:<expect>` or a verified equivalent. Background fetch can weaken a bare lease. On lease failure, query the change and reassess; do not merely refresh the expected SHA and retry blindly, or escalate to bare `--force`. A lease guards an expected version and grants no permission.

Do not move, overwrite, or delete published tags without authorization. Publish corrections under the project's version policy. A necessary withdrawal or history cleanup first specifies approval scope, affected consumers, and recovery. Verify intended behavior and the final version after a revert.
